About

How can we evaluate and improve safety when a driving policy's actions determine its future observations? A driving policy must do more than predict a plausible trajectory from a recorded image. It must respond to observations that its own actions create.

This full-day workshop brings together researchers in computer vision, robotics, simulation, and transportation to study closed-loop safety. It connects safety-critical simulation, closed-loop learning, safe planning, recovery, and safety assurance. The program combines invited talks, contributed papers, posters, a reproducible closed-loop safety challenge, an interactive failure clinic, and a panel on safety evidence. Together, participants will develop shared recommendations for diagnosing safety failures and measuring closed-loop improvements across driving policies and simulation platforms.

The workshop is motivated by NavSafe-∞, a benchmark of 280 photorealistic scenarios that cover 28 safety-critical event types in four categories: traffic crashes, vulnerable road user crashes, traffic violations, and traffic incidents. Its evaluation of 20 driving policies finds that open-loop improvements do not reliably predict closed-loop safety.

Topics

We welcome work on closed-loop safety in end-to-end and modular driving systems, vision-language-action models, learned world models, and classical planning baselines. Contributions must connect their methods to safety during policy–environment interaction. Work may use NavSafe-∞ or other evaluation resources.

  • Closed-loop safety measurement and generalization Event-level metrics, uncertainty estimates, held-out evaluation, benchmark contamination, and how open-loop, pseudo-simulation, and interactive evaluation relate.
  • Safety-critical closed-loop simulation Neural reconstruction, 3D Gaussian splatting, generative simulation, camera calibration, temporal consistency, reactive traffic, and counterfactual safety-event editing.
  • Closed-loop learning, safe planning, and recovery Interactive data collection, imitation and reinforcement learning, safety-aware reward design, uncertainty-aware planning, runtime safeguards, and recovery-aware supervision.
  • Safety-event design and failure diagnosis Vulnerable road users, occlusion, traffic-rule compliance, rare interactions, scenario coverage, perception–planning interfaces, and reproducible rollout analysis. Negative results are in scope.
  • Safety assurance What evidence supports a safety claim beyond a benchmark score?

Outcomes

  • A closed-loop safety evaluation checklist: observations, simulator assumptions, data splits, seeds, safety metrics, and compute.
  • Diagnostic safety case studies, shared with author consent.
  • A community report on consensus and open safety-assurance questions.

Call for Papers

We invite papers on all topics of the workshop. Acceptance does not depend on using NavSafe-∞, entering the challenge, or reporting a positive result.

Archival

Full papers

Up to 8 pages, excluding references

Published in the CVPR 2027 workshop proceedings.

Non-archival

Extended abstracts

2–4 pages, excluding references

Work in progress, negative results, position papers, and reproducibility studies are welcome.

Format CVPR 2027 template and policies. Authors choose the track at submission.
Review Double-blind on OpenReview, with at least three reviews per paper.
Review criteria Technical quality, relevance to closed-loop safety, clarity of safety claims and evaluation assumptions, reproducibility, and potential to stimulate discussion.
Presentation All accepted papers get a poster. Selected papers get an oral talk or a spotlight.
Submission portal OpenReview link to be announced

NavSafe-∞ Closed-Loop Safety Challenge

The challenge tests whether driving policies handle safety-critical visual driving events. Teams submit a policy. We run it in closed loop through photorealistic, neurally reconstructed scenarios and score how it handles each safety event. The results give the workshop shared, inspectable evidence.

NavSafe-∞ project page Paper (arXiv) Devkit: Feb 1, 2027
280public scenarios
28safety-critical event types
140private instances, at least 5 per type
3fixed seeds per event

Evaluation pools

Every entry is evaluated on two scenario pools.

Released

Public pool

280 published scenarios, 28 event types

The NavSafe-∞ benchmark. Use it to validate your policy and to compare with the 20 baselines in the paper. Because these events are public, the public pool does not decide the ranking.

Used forValidation and comparison

Held by the organizers

Private pool

At least 5 instances per event type (140 in total)

New instances of the same 28 event types, never released. Source logs and near-duplicate scenes are kept apart from the public pool and from public training data.

Used forFinal ranking and awards

Policy interface

Input

Rendered camera observations, calibration, permitted ego-state history, and a route instruction.

Your policy

A versioned container, run on organizer-controlled workers without network access.

Output

A future ego trajectory.

The camera rig, observation history, controller, and vehicle model are the same for all entries.

Training data

Any publicly available dataset or pretrained model, including but not limited to NAVSIM/OpenScene and nuPlan. All training data must be declared at submission. The public pool is reserved for validation.

Not allowed

  • LiDAR
  • Privileged scene state
  • Ground-truth object tracks
  • Future actor trajectories
  • The logged future ego trajectory
  • Training or tuning on either pool, evaluation logs, or score feedback

Evaluation and ranking

Ranking order

  1. Mean Driving Score over all private-pool events and three fixed seeds
  2. If tied: Success Rate
  3. If still tied: the higher worst-category ability score

Reported for every entry

  • Driving Score (DS)
  • Success Rate (SR)
  • Driving Efficiency (DE)
  • Comfort

Also reported

  • Four category ability scores
  • Per-event-type results
  • Collisions and violations
  • Seed variation
  • The devkit uses the event-specific criteria from the NavSafe-∞ paper.
  • Public-pool results use the published aggregation, so they are comparable with the paper.
  • A human driver is evaluated on the private pool with the same interface, as a reference.

Submission and rules

Submit
A versioned container and model checksum. Submit through the challenge portal on Hugging Face (opens March 1, 2027).
Limits
One validation submission per team per day, ten in total, and two final models.
Feedback
Private-pool scores are withheld until the deadline.
Verification
Top entries send a training-data declaration and a technical report, and pass an independent rerun before awards.
Eligibility
Organizer entries are reference only and cannot win awards.
Compute
GPU type, memory cap, per-step timeout, and container size limits will be published at launch.
